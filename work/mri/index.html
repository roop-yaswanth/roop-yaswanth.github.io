The question

Can a model fill
the gap between slices?

Prostate MRI trades detail between slices for scan time.

Clinical T2-weighted scans are sharp within a slice (0.547 mm) but coarse between slices (1.5 mm). Thinner slices need longer scans, which bring motion artifacts and patient discomfort, and the gaps limit 3D analysis of a tumour.

A single slice can’t recover detail it never captured. Multi-image super-resolution reads the neighbouring slices instead: given the slices above and below, predict the one in between.

The data

782 patients,
split by patient.

No patient’s scans appear in both training and testing.

From TCIA’s Prostate-MRI-US-Biopsy collection, I used the axial T2-weighted series of 782 patients and split them 70/15/15 at the patient level: 547 for training, 117 for validation and 118 for testing, with 8,587 test slices in all.

Slices were cropped to 256 × 256, scaled to [0, 1] between the 1st and 99th intensity percentiles (z-scored for the residual network), and augmented with flips and 90° rotations.

The models

Three models,
one missing slice.

Each reads the neighbouring slices and predicts the one between them.

Residual CNN

0.78M parameters. Two neighbouring slices in, ten residual blocks of 64 feature maps, trained on L1, gradient and SSIM losses.

SRGAN

A 4.43M-parameter generator with 24 residual blocks against a 5.21M discriminator. Five epochs of L1 and SSIM pretraining, then 20 adversarial epochs with a VGG perceptual loss.

SwinIR

3.47M parameters. Four slices in, two on each side, through four residual Swin Transformer blocks with shifted-window attention, trained on a loss weighted toward SSIM.

The results

Higher average fidelity.
Lower variation.

Three model configurations, evaluated on the same 8,587 held-out slices.

PSNR measures pixel accuracy in decibels; SSIM measures structural similarity, from 0 to 1. Each figure is a mean with its standard deviation across slices. SwinIR uses four input slices; the other models use two. Losses and normalization also differ.

Error analysis

Where each
model fails.

Error maps show different failures, not just different scores.

The residual CNN’s errors trace high-contrast edges such as the prostate capsule, where it blurs. SRGAN’s spread across the slice as texture the scan doesn’t have. SwinIR’s are the smallest and stay in the most complex anatomy.

On a 172-slice sample, SwinIR was the best of the three on 82% of slices, SRGAN on 12% and the residual CNN on 6%. Where SRGAN or the residual CNN won most clearly, the slice was dominated by a large, bright, uniform region; on one, SwinIR trailed by 6.3 dB.

What I take forward

A stronger reconstruction.
A clear next experiment.

The four-slice SwinIR configuration achieved the highest average reconstruction scores.

It reached 28.18 dB PSNR and 0.925 SSIM. Matching the input context, loss and normalization in a follow-up experiment would isolate which changes account for the gain.

The open question is downstream: whether the rebuilt slices improve prostate segmentation.
