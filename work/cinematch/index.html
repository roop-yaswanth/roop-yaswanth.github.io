The approach

Two retrievers.
One fused shortlist.

Multilingual semantic search and graph collaborative filtering each score the catalog; late fusion, a quality gate and DPP reranking turn them into ten picks.

A simplified view of the CineMatch pipeline.

Why semantic retrieval?

It represents films directly from their descriptions, so newly indexed titles can be retrieved before they receive ratings.

Why collaborative filtering?

It carries the relevance. On films people have already rated, shared taste is the strongest signal.

Why diversify?

Highly similar results make a repetitive list. DPP reranking turns diversity into an explicit, tunable setting with a known cost.

Search by meaning

BGE-M3 represents film descriptions in a shared multilingual space, supporting semantic queries across the catalog.

Personalize from ratings

XSimGCL learns user and film representations from the rating graph; late fusion combines taste with semantic relevance.

Include unrated films

The semantic path can retrieve titles without interaction history, so discovery is not limited to the collaborative model’s rated items.

Evaluation methodology

A global chronological split: models train on every rating up to 5 November 2020, and test users are drawn from the 3.2 million ratings that came after. Each recommendation is ranked against all 65,723 titles in the frozen catalog. Comparisons use per-user paired bootstrap 95% intervals and Wilcoxon tests, with Holm correction across 32 prespecified tests.

  • Frozen fusion reached NDCG@10 of 0.0664 on the primary 2,500-user test. The semantic index’s 1.37M-title serving catalog is separate from the 65,723-title evaluation catalog.
  • Frozen fusion, language bridging and DPP reranking are evaluated as separate configurations. Language exposure is measured on a 1,013-user cross-language cohort.
  • Cold-start evaluation separately tests titles with no training interactions and users arriving after the training cutoff.

The delivery

Beyond the
ranking model.

A useful recommender also needs dependable routes from the browser to the data, clear service boundaries, and a way to keep its catalog current.

Bounded API requests

The Next.js proxy limits forwarded paths and headers, caps request bodies at 1 MB and applies separate rate limits to login and general requests.

Next.js and FastAPI

Search across sources

A search route combines catalog, TMDB and IMDb matches, ranks titles by relevance and merges overlapping results into one response.

Search and metadata integration

Incremental catalog updates

The update pipeline encodes newly fetched titles and appends them to the search index, allowing the catalog to grow without re-encoding every existing film.

What I take forward

Discovery is
a design decision.

A ranking score can’t describe the whole shortlist.

This project brings relevance, catalog coverage, language exposure and engineering behavior into the same discussion. The results point to a serving policy rather than one ranker: taste-led fusion for people with history, averaged item vectors for newcomers, and semantic retrieval wherever new or less-seen films matter.
